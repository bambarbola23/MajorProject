# Theory & Report Material: Vertical EOG Electrode Placement and Physiological Principles

*This document explains the biological, electrical, and physical principles behind Vertical Electrooculography (EOG) electrode placement. This is designed for your Major Project Report and Viva/Defense.*

## 1. The Physiological Principle: The Eye as a Dipole

The human eye behaves as an electrical generator that creates a constant dipole field. 
* **The Dipole Structure:** The **cornea** (the front of the eye) is electrically **positive** relative to the **retina** (the back of the eye), which is electrically **negative**. This is due to the high metabolic activity in the retina and the chemical characteristics of the eyeball's layers.
* **Corneo-Retinal Potential (CRP):** This potential difference ranges between **0.4 mV and 1.0 mV**.
* **Movement Detection:** When the eye moves, this dipole rotates. By placing electrodes on the skin around the eye socket, we can detect the movement of this electrical dipole field.

## 3. Why are they placed particularly there?

### A. The Geometry of a Blink (Bell's Phenomenon)
When a person blinks:
1. The eyelid moves down across the eye, acting as a temporary conductor (shunt) between the cornea and the skin.
2. At the same time, a physiological reflex known as **Bell's Phenomenon** occurs: **the eyeball involuntarily rotates upward** to protect the cornea.
3. Because the eyeball rotates upward, the positive pole (cornea) rotates closer to the **IN+ (Red)** electrode on the forehead, and further away from the **IN- (White)** electrode on the cheekbone.
4. This rapid rotation creates a sharp **positive voltage spike** in the differential reading: `IN+ - IN-`.
5. When the eye opens back up, the eyeball rotates down to its neutral position, creating a corresponding **negative recovery wave**.

### B. The Differential Mode (Common Mode Rejection)
* The electrical potential of a blink is small (tens to hundreds of microvolts at the skin surface).
* The human body acts as an antenna, picking up a massive **50Hz power line noise** from lights and wiring.
* Because the **IN+** and **IN-** electrodes are placed close together on the face, they both receive the exact same 50Hz noise (known as **Common-Mode Noise**).
* The **REF (Black)** electrode is placed on a neutral bone (the mastoid process behind the ear). Since it is on a bone, it picks up no muscle activity, only the common-mode electrical noise.
* The BioAmp EXG Pill's instrumentation amplifier performs subtraction: 
  $$\text{Output Voltage} = (\text{IN+} - \text{REF}) - (\text{IN-} - \text{REF}) = \text{IN+} - \text{IN-}$$
  This effectively cancels out the 50Hz noise and extracts the clean vertical eye movement signal.

## 4. Key Terms for your Project Report

* **Electrooculography (EOG):** A technique for measuring the corneo-retinal standing potential that exists between the front and the back of the human eye.
* **Corneo-Retinal Dipole:** The electric field generated by the positive cornea and the negative retina.
* **Bell's Phenomenon:** The upward and outward movement of the eye that occurs when an individual blinks or closes their eyes.
* **Differential Amplification:** The amplification of the difference between two input voltages while rejecting any signals common to both inputs.
